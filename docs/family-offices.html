The family office is the one firm
the register was built to miss.

You asked for a list of family offices — in Europe, the United States, Hong Kong and beyond. Here is the licence map for nine jurisdictions, and the reason a public register can hand you that list almost nowhere: a family office earns its exemption precisely by serving only its own family, and what is exempt never files. The licensed ones that do file hide inside generic categories. Two registers are the exception — and their rosters are below.

Who needs what

Nobody licenses “a family office.” They license what it does.

Every regime turns on one hinge: are you serving only your own family, or someone else’s? A single family office is, almost everywhere, exempt. The instant it takes on a second, unrelated family — or holds itself out to the public — the licence attaches to the activity (managing investments, advising, dealing), not to the words “family office”.

The blind spot

Why “a list of family offices” is a question the register cannot answer.

This is the Ledger’s thesis run in reverse. A register is trustworthy because no licensed firm can choose not to appear in it — filing is a condition of trading. The family office is the counter-example, designed into the gap:

The exempt do not file, and the licensed are not labelled.

A single family office qualifies for its exemption by not serving third parties — so it has no obligation to file, and appears in no register at all. A multi-family office that does need a licence files under a generic heading — MiFID investment firm, AIFM, SFC Type 9, an SEC-registered adviser — with no field that says “family office.” Either way the register cannot single them out.

So the honest count of “family offices in a public register” is: the handful of jurisdictions that made the label itself a registrable thing. That is Luxembourg (as a licence) and, indirectly, the United States and the UAE. Everywhere else, the list you want is the population the register was built not to hold.

Roster · by name

United States: the ones that grew large enough to have to file.

The SEC does not register family offices — its 2011 rule excludes them. But an office managing more than $100m in US-listed securities must file a Form 13F, and EDGAR’s full-text search will return every filer whose registered name contains “family office”. That is the list below: 23 firms, ranked by how many 13F reports they have filed. Roster · registered, not licensed

The UAE registers the family office — even when it needs no licence.

Dubai’s DIFC and Abu Dhabi’s ADGM are unusual: a pure single family office needs no financial-services licence, yet it is still an entity on a public register — the DIFC Registrar of Companies (via the Family Wealth Centre) or the ADGM Registration Authority. So a roster does in principle exist; both portals, however, rate-limit automated collection, so they are linked rather than reproduced here.

Reproduce

Every name here came from the register itself.

No commercial “top family offices” list was used — those are voluntary, motivated and edited, the very sources the Ledger sets aside. Both rosters are one request each.

# Luxembourg — the 5 licensed family offices (CSSF specialised PFS)
curl -s 'https://edesk.apps.cssf.lu/search-entities-api/api/v1/entite?size=500&entType=SPP' \
  # then keep entities whose detail has ACTIVITY_CODE = ACT_SPP_FO

# United States — 13F filers named "family office" (SEC EDGAR full-text search)
curl -s 'https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index?q=%22family+office%22&forms=13F-HR' \
  # read aggregations.entity_filter; keep names containing "family office"
